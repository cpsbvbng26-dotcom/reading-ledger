## A reduction found during the check (my own check, not a source)

Write y = δx, β = 1/δ and t(β) = (2 − β)/(β − 1) = δX. In the window, t < 1. Then

    S = δ⁻¹ · K,   K = { y ∈ [0,1) : T_β^n(y) ≤ t(β) for all n ≥ 0 },

where T_β(y) = βy mod 1 is the greedy β-transformation. In words, S is the survivor set of the
greedy β-transformation with the hole (t(β), 1) at the right end. Equivalently, it is the hole
(δ²X, δ) = (t/β, 1/β), whose right end is the discontinuity 1/β. In the digit coding x = Σ aᵢδⁱ,
the itineraries are { a : every tail value ≤ X }, a one-sided "lexicographic world". Up to a
countable set this is the β′-shift { a : σⁿa ≼ w }, and Prop 9's equation Σ_{n∈A_δ} z^n = 1 is
Parry's equation 1 = Σ wᵢ β′^{−i} with β′ = β^dim.

Checks (scratch scripts; nothing published):
- Membership: 50,000 random points at each of δ = 0.62, 0.63, 0.64, 0.65, 0.66, 0.6666, depth 25.
  The game recursion and the greedy-map criterion agreed on every point (0 mismatches).
- Entropy: word counts of {σⁿa ≼ w} give dimension estimates 0.608, 0.722, 0.826, 0.920 and 0.999 at
  δ = 0.63 … 0.6666, matching Prop 9's table. At δ = 0.62 the finite-depth estimate 0.368 is below the
  proved 0.404, so the count converges slowly there.
- At δ = 0.64 the first-return words are {0, 1000}, A_δ = {1, 4}, and z + z⁴ = 1. This is the
  β′-shift whose greedy expansion of 1 is 1001.
- h(δ) has plateaus: with A_δ = {1, 4}, h = 0.322285 on the whole of δ ∈ [0.6400, 0.6450].
  Meanwhile dim = h/log β rises strictly, from 0.722146 to 0.734962.
- Endpoints: at β = φ, t = 1/φ, the bound is 10^∞, and the set is countable (Prop 5(3)).
  At β = 3/2, t = 1, there is no hole.

## Item 1. Prop 8 (Lebesgue measure zero)

Verdict: known in substance. It is an immediate corollary of classical results, though no source was
found that states it for this exact set.

Reason: by the reduction, S is a survivor set of T_β with a hole of positive length. T_β has an
invariant measure equivalent to Lebesgue and is ergodic for it (Rényi 1957; Parry 1960). So almost
every orbit enters the hole, and the survivor set is null. Prop 9's dim < 1 also implies Prop 8.
The note's density-point proof is correct, as far as I can see, but it rederives a standard fact.

  Academiae Scientiarum Hungaricae. DOI 10.1007/bf02020331. OpenAlex W2089164015.
  No abstract was retrievable. The ergodicity statement is recalled, not seen.
- Parry, W. (1960). On the β-expansions of real numbers. Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar.
  DOI 10.1007/bf02020954. OpenAlex W2075595154. No abstract was retrievable; statement not seen.
- Hofbauer, F. (1978). β-shifts have unique maximal measure. Monatshefte für Mathematik.
  DOI 10.1007/bf01534862. OpenAlex W1984240570. No abstract was retrievable. The title supports dim < 1
  for proper subshifts that miss a cylinder. That inference is mine.
- Kong, D., Sun, B., Wang, Z. (2026). Open dynamical systems with a moving hole. Advances in Mathematics.

## Item 2. Prop 9's dimension formula, and dim = h / log β

Verdict: partly known. The general principle dim = h_top/log β is standard, and the formula is
Parry's entropy equation for a lexicographically bounded shift. Formulas of the same kind exist for
neighbouring holes. This exact closed form for the hole (t(β), 1) was not seen in any abstract.

Sources:
- Hofbauer, F., Raith, P. (1992). The Hausdorff dimension of an ergodic invariant measure for a
  piecewise monotonic map of the interval. Canadian Math. Bulletin. DOI 10.4153/cmb-1992-013-x.
  OpenAlex W1965197670. Abstract: "we show for an ergodic invariant measure μ with positive Ljapunov
  exponent λμ that the Hausdorff dimension of μ equals hμ / λμ." This is the measure version. Taking
  the maximal-entropy measure gives dim ≥ h/log β; the cover bound gives ≤.
- Raith, P. (1989). Hausdorff dimension for piecewise monotonic maps. Studia Mathematica.
  DOI 10.4064/sm-94-1-17-33. OpenAlex W2031162696. Likely the set version (dim of an invariant subset
  via pressure); no abstract was retrievable, statement not seen.
- Urbański, M. (1986). On Hausdorff dimension of invariant sets for expanding maps of a circle. ETDS.
  DOI 10.1017/s0143385700003461. OpenAlex W2096804511. Abstract: "We prove that topological entropy of
  g|K_g(ε) is a Cantor function of ε." It also says: "In § 3 we establish some relationships between
  the mappings g|K_g(ε) and the theory of β-transformations".
- Kuang, R., Li, B., Xiao, Y. (2026). The Hausdorff dimension of the survivor set. arXiv preprint.
  DOI 10.48550/arxiv.2604.23737. OpenAlex W7157023735. For the hole (0,t), the abstract states:
  "The Hausdorff dimension of the survivor set K(t) … is equal to −ln λ/ln β … where λ ∈ (0,1) is the
  smallest positive solution of the equation Σ_{n=1}^∞ (α(β)_n − t_n) x^n = 1". This is the same
  generating-function type as Prop 9, for the hole at the other end.
- Sun, Y., Li, B., Ding, Y. (2024). Topological expansive Lorenz maps with a hole at critical point.
  Journal of Statistical Physics. DOI 10.1007/s10955-024-03265-0 (arXiv 10.48550/arxiv.2311.02465).
  OpenAlex W4395023557. Abstract (arXiv): "We show that the case a=c is equivalent to the hole at 0,
  the case b=c equals to the hole at 1." It also states "using the Ledrappier-Young formula, we obtain
  that the Hausdorff dimension function … is a devil staircase". Our hole (t/β, 1/β) is exactly
  their case b = c for the greedy β-map, which is an intermediate β-transformation.
- Lu, J., Steiner, W., Zou, Y. (2026). Hausdorff dimension of double-base expansions and binary shifts
  with a hole. Bulletin of the London Math. Society. DOI 10.1112/blms.70344
  (arXiv 10.48550/arxiv.2509.04227). OpenAlex W7144164174. Abstract: "We derive explicit formulas for
  the Hausdorff dimension … and the entropy of the underlying subshift … Our results also concern
  general dynamical systems described by binary shifts with a hole, including, in particular, the
  doubling map with a hole and (linear) Lorenz maps." Likely relevant; statement for our case not seen.
- Parry 1960 (above): entropy of the β′-shift is log β′. Recalled, not seen.

Needs full text: Lu–Steiner–Zou (does their formula cover a linear Lorenz map with a hole ending at
the discontinuity, with slope β?). Also Sun–Li–Ding (is the dimension formula stated or only the
staircase?), and Raith 1989.

## Item 3. Continuity, monotonicity, devil's staircase; β and the hole moving together

Verdict: partly known. For a fixed β, the devil's staircase in the hole parameter is known for
neighbouring holes. Joint dependence on (β, t) is known to be discontinuous for the hole at 0. For
the specific curve t = (2 − β)/(β − 1), nothing was found. The closest analogue, where base and
constraint move together, is the univoque set.

Sources:
- Kalle et al. (2020), above, as reported in Allaart and Kong (2022): "Kalle et al … proved that the
  Hausdorff dimension function t ↦ dim_H K_β(t) is a non-increasing Devil's staircase."
- Allaart, P., Kong, D. (2023). Critical values for the β-transformation with a hole at 0. ETDS.
  DOI 10.1017/etds.2022.24 (arXiv 10.48550/arxiv.2109.10012). OpenAlex W4225287953. Abstract:
  "Consequently, the dimension dim_H K_β(t) is not jointly continuous in β and t." The arXiv record also
  says "We corrected Remark 1.10 on the discontinuity of the joint map (β, t) → dim_H K_β(t)".
  CORRECTED after full-text reading (see below): arXiv v2, Remark 1.10, withdraws the published claim.
  It says joint continuity is suspected but unproved. So joint continuity is open, not known to fail.
  Continuity along a moving curve still does not follow from any stated theorem. Curiosity: the same abstract gives τ(β) = (2 − β)/(β − 1) at the Komornik–Loreti
  constant. That is the same expression as our t(β), but for the hole at 0 and outside our window.
- Allaart, P., Kong, D. (2025). The β-transformation with a hole at 0: the general case. ETDS.

## Judgment: what looks new

- Prop 8 is not new as mathematics. It follows from the ergodicity of T_β (Rényi, Parry) once S is
  recognised as a survivor set with a hole of positive length. It also follows from Prop 9's dim < 1.
- Prop 9's formula is not new as a type of theorem. It combines dim = h/log β (Raith; Hofbauer–Raith)
  with Parry's entropy of a lexicographically bounded shift. Close relatives are stated for the hole
  at 0 (Kuang–Li–Xiao 2026) and for Lorenz maps with a hole at the critical point (Sun–Li–Ding 2024;
  Lu–Steiner–Zou 2026). It may be contained verbatim in Lu–Steiner–Zou or Sun–Li–Ding; that was not seen.
- What was not found anywhere:
  (a) the reduction of this game's pure-strategy set to the greedy β-map with hole (t(β), 1),
      t(β) = (2 − β)/(β − 1);
  (b) continuity, strict monotonicity and the limits 0 and 1 along this coupled curve, where base and
      hole move together. Joint continuity in (β, t) is open (Allaart–Kong arXiv v2, Remark 1.10;
      corrected below), so a curve-specific proof has content;
  (c) an exact dimension, and measure zero, for an explicit pure-strategy equilibrium payoff set.
      Berg–Kitti 2014 give an estimation method only, per their abstract.
- The likely contribution is small and is the combination: an explicit game example where the
  dimension is exact and strictly increasing in δ. The entropy has plateaus, a devil's-staircase
  feature, and the moving base removes them, as for univoque sets (Komornik–Kong–Li 2017). The proofs
  of monotonicity (nested subshifts in δ) and continuity are elementary. Before calling them new, the
  full texts of Lu–Steiner–Zou 2026, Sun–Li–Ding 2024, Berg–Kitti 2014 and Raith 1992/1994 would have
  to be checked.

## Full-text reading (arXiv)

Done on 2026-11-18, after arxiv.org and export.arxiv.org became reachable. The PDFs were saved in
`scratchpad/arxiv-dl/` and converted with `pdftotext -layout` into `scratchpad/arxiv-txt/`.

Our case throughout: the greedy β-map with the hole (t(β), 1), t(β) = (2−β)/(β−1), 3/2 < β < φ, with β
and t moving together. Symbolically, with π(i) = Σ i_k β^{−k}, the itinerary set is
{u : σⁿu ≼ w for all n}, where w is the quasi-greedy β-expansion of t(β). Up to a countable set this
equals Ω_{0w, 10^∞} = {u : no tail lies in ]0w, 10^∞[}; the hole in values is (t/β, 1/β). The identity
Ω_{0a′,1b′} = ⋃ 0ⁿΣ_{b′,a′} ∪ ⋃ 1ⁿΣ_{b′,a′} ∪ {0^∞, 1^∞} used for this is stated as (1.9) in Allaart–Kong
2304.06892v3, citing Komornik–Steiner–Zou.

### 1. Lu, Steiner, Zou — arXiv 2509.04227v2 (21 Feb 2026); published DOI 10.1112/blms.70344

Read in full: Sections 1–3 (statements and proofs of Theorems 1.1, 1.2, 1.4, 1.5; Lemmas 2.1, 3.6, 3.16;
Propositions 2.5, 2.6).

- Theorem 1.2: "Let (a, b) ∈ W and q0, q1 > 1. (i) If π_{β,β}(a) < π_{β,β}(b) for all β > 1, then
  h(Ω_{a,b}) = 0 = dim_H π_{q0,q1}(Ω_{a,b}). (ii) Otherwise, we have h(Ω_{a,b}) = log β for the maximal
  β > 1 such that π_{β,β}(a) = π_{β,β}(b), dim_H π_{q0,q1}(Ω_{a,b}) = min{1, s} for the maximal s > 0
  s.t. π_{q0^s,q1^s}(a) = π_{q0^s,q1^s}(b)." Here W = {(a,b) ∈ 0{0,1}^∞ × 1{0,1}^∞ : a, b ∈ Ω_{a,b}}, and
  "if a or b is not in Ω_{a,b}, then we can replace (a, b) by (ℓ(a, b), r(a, b))" (Lemma 2.7:
  Ω_{ℓ(a,b),r(a,b)} = Ω_{a,b}).
- Proposition 2.5: "We have d_{q0,q1}(a, b) = 0 if and only if h(Ω_{a,b}) = 0,
  d_{q0,q1}(a, b) = 1 if and only if π_{q0,q1}(Ω_{a,b}) = [0, 1/(q1−1)]." Its proof adds: "If
  max π_{q0,q1}([0]_{a,b}) < min π_{q0,q1}([1]_{a,b}) … note that s < 1 in this case".
- Theorem 1.4: "Let q0, q1 > 1. Then the function d_{q0,q1} is continuous" (in (a, b), product topology).
- Lemma 3.16: "Let a ∈ 0{0,1}^∞, b ∈ 0{0,1}^∞. Then the map (q0, q1) ↦ d_{q0,q1}(a, b) is continuous for
  q0, q1 > 1." The second "0{0,1}^∞" is printed so; 1{0,1}^∞ is presumably meant.
- Theorem 1.5 (continuity of (q0,q1) ↦ dim_H U_{q0,q1}) is proved by combining Theorem 1.4 and Lemma 3.16
  along the curve (q0,q1) ↦ (a_{q0,q1}, b_{q0,q1}). The text handles the jumps of the quasi-greedy
  expansion at periodic points by Lemma 3.2.

For our case, take q0 = q1 = β, a = ℓ(0w, 10^∞) and b = 10^∞.
- Prop 9 formula: directly implied. The equation π_{β^s}(a) = π_{β^s}(10^∞) = β^{−s} reads
  Σ_k u_k β^{−sk} = 1, with a = 0u. This is Prop 9's equation, with A_δ = the positions of 1 in u.
  Check at δ = 0.64: w = 10001001…, ℓ gives u = (1000)^∞, the equation is z + z⁴ = 1, and this matches
  A_δ = {1, 4}. Theorem 1.2 also gives h = log β′, with β′ the maximal root of the same equation in
  base β′. The paper gives the Hausdorff dimension only; Prop 9's claim dim_B = dim_H is not stated there.
- dim < 1: directly implied by Proposition 2.5. In our case max π([0]) ≤ t/β < 1/β ≤ min π([1]).
- Prop 8: follows from dim < 1.
- Continuity along our curve: not stated. Theorem 1.4 fixes the base; Lemma 3.16 fixes (a, b). Their
  combination is carried out only for the univoque curve (Theorem 1.5). The same combination plausibly
  works for our curve (my inference, not proved there).
- Strict monotonicity in δ, and the limits at 1/φ and 2/3: not stated.

## Final verdicts after full-text reading

1. Prop 8 (measure zero): known.
   It is an immediate special case of Lu–Steiner–Zou, Prop 2.5 (dim < 1 when the two cylinder images are
   separated, as here), and also of the ergodicity of T_β (Rényi, Parry; not read).
2. Prop 9:
   - The formula Σ_{n∈A_δ} δ^{ns} = 1 is known as a special case of Lu–Steiner–Zou, Theorem 1.2 (with
     Lemma 2.7), for q0 = q1 = β and (a, b) = (ℓ(0w, 10^∞), 10^∞). The entropy part is also Kuang–Li–Xiao,
     Theorem 4.3, with t = 0^∞. dim = h/log β is standard (quoted from Raith in Sun–Li–Ding).
   - dim < 1 is known (Lu–Steiner–Zou, Prop 2.5).
   - Not found: dim_B = dim_H (not stated in the papers read). The note's own first-return proof is new
     in form only.
3. Continuity, strict monotonicity and limits along the curve: partly known.
   - Known: continuity in the symbolic hole for a fixed base (Lu–Steiner–Zou Thm 1.4), continuity in the
     base for a fixed symbolic hole (their Lemma 3.16), and the devil's staircase for a fixed β with the
     hole moving (Sun–Li–Ding, Cor. 1.5 and the "b fixed" remark).
   - Known, for the hole at 0: continuity in β for each fixed t, stated without proof ("not difficult to
     show") by Allaart–Kong v2, Remark 1.10. Joint continuity in (β, t) is open there.
   - Not found after full-text reading: continuity along our curve t = (2−β)/(β−1), strict monotonicity
     in δ, and the limits 0 at 1/φ and 1 at 2/3. Lu–Steiner–Zou's Theorem 1.5 argument would plausibly
     transfer to the continuity statement. Strict monotonicity and the limits have no counterpart in
     what was read.
4. Game application: partly known (unchanged). The phenomenon and a dimension-estimation method are in
   Berg–Kitti 2014 (abstract). Their papers and Mailath–Obara–Sekiguchi are not on arXiv and were not
   read in full. An exact dimension or measure zero for this segment was not found.
5. Props 1–7: not found (unchanged; no full text bears on them).

What looks new after this reading:
- (a) The identification of the game's pure-strategy set with the survivor set of T_β with the hole
  (t(β), 1), t(β) = (2−β)/(β−1), so that the known theorems apply.
- (b) Strict monotonicity, continuity and the endpoint limits along this coupled curve. Monotonicity
  rests on a simple nesting argument.
- (c) Its use as an exact computation in a repeated game.
- Props 8 and 9 as stated, apart from monotonicity, continuity and limits, should be cited as
  special cases of Lu–Steiner–Zou (2026) and of the Rényi–Parry theory, not claimed.
